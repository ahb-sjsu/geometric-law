## A.1 The Dihedral Group $D_4$

**Definition A.1 (Dihedral Group).** The *dihedral group* $D_n$ is the symmetry group of a regular $n$-gon. It has $2n$ elements: $n$ rotations and $n$ reflections. The group is generated by a rotation $r$ of order $n$ and a reflection $s$ of order 2, subject to the relation $srs = r^{-1}$.

**Definition A.2 ($D_4$ Presentation).** The dihedral group $D_4$ has the presentation:

$$D_4 = \langle r, s \mid r^4 = s^2 = e, \; srs = r^{-1} \rangle$$

with 8 elements: $\{e, r, r^2, r^3, s, sr, sr^2, sr^3\}$.

*Legal interpretation*: $D_4$ is the symmetry group of Hohfeld's first square of jural relations (Chapter 2). The four positions --- right, duty, liberty, no-right --- sit at the vertices of a square. The rotation $r$ cycles them: Right $\to$ Duty $\to$ Liberty $\to$ No-right $\to$ Right. The reflection $s$ swaps opposites: Right $\leftrightarrow$ No-right, Duty $\leftrightarrow$ Liberty. The eight group elements correspond to eight ways of relabeling the Hohfeldian positions while preserving their relational structure.

**Definition A.3 (Semidirect Product).** Given groups $N$ and $H$ and a homomorphism $\varphi: H \to \text{Aut}(N)$, the *semidirect product* $N \rtimes_\varphi H$ is the set $N \times H$ with multiplication $(n_1, h_1)(n_2, h_2) = (n_1 \cdot \varphi(h_1)(n_2), \; h_1 h_2)$.

*Legal interpretation*: The full Hohfeldian gauge group is $D_4 \rtimes D_4$ (Chapter 5). The first $D_4$ acts on the first square (right, duty, liberty, no-right); the second $D_4$ acts on the second square (power, liability, immunity, disability). The semidirect product encodes the fact that second-order positions (powers and immunities) can modify first-order positions (rights and duties), introducing a coupling between the two squares.

## A.2 Simplicial Complexes and Path Homology

**Definition A.4 (Simplicial Complex).** A *simplicial complex* $K$ is a collection of simplices (vertices, edges, triangles, ...) closed under taking faces. A $k$-simplex is the convex hull of $k+1$ affinely independent points.

**Definition A.5 (Directed Simplicial Complex).** A *directed simplicial complex* is a simplicial complex in which each 1-simplex (edge) has a designated source and target vertex, and higher simplices inherit a consistent orientation.

**Definition A.6 (Weighted Simplicial Complex).** A *weighted simplicial complex* is a simplicial complex equipped with a weight function $w: K_1 \to \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ on its 1-simplices (edges).

*Legal interpretation*: The judicial complex $\mathcal{K}$ (Chapter 3) is a weighted directed simplicial complex. Vertices are decided cases. Directed edges are doctrinal relationships (case A cites case B, case A overrules case B). Edge weights are the Mahalanobis distance between the connected cases plus boundary penalties.

**Definition A.7 (Path Homology).** For a directed graph $G = (V, E)$, define the *path chain complex*:
- $\Omega_0(G)$ is the free abelian group generated by vertices.
- $\Omega_1(G)$ is the free abelian group generated by directed edges.
- $\Omega_n(G)$ is generated by directed paths of length $n$.
- The boundary operator $\partial_n: \Omega_n \to \Omega_{n-1}$ removes intermediate vertices.

The *path homology groups* are $\widetilde{H}_n^{\text{path}}(G) = \ker(\partial_n) / \text{im}(\partial_{n+1})$.

*Legal interpretation*: Path homology measures the topological structure of the citation network (Chapter 7). A non-trivial element of $\widetilde{H}_1^{\text{path}}$ represents a "hole" in the legal network --- a cycle of citations that cannot be filled by intermediate cases. Constitutional review checks whether adding a statute changes the path homology of the constitutional subcomplex.

## A.3 The Mahalanobis Distance

**Definition A.8 (Mahalanobis Distance).** Given a positive-definite covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, the *Mahalanobis distance* between vectors $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is:

$$d_M(x, y) = \sqrt{(x - y)^T \Sigma^{-1} (x - y)}$$

**Properties:**
- When $\Sigma = I$ (identity), the Mahalanobis distance reduces to Euclidean distance.
- The Mahalanobis distance accounts for correlations between dimensions: if two dimensions covary strongly, changes along their joint direction have lower cost than changes along independent directions.
- The Mahalanobis distance is invariant under affine transformations that preserve $\Sigma$.

*Legal interpretation*: The Mahalanobis distance is the natural metric on the judicial manifold (Chapter 4). The $8 \times 8$ covariance matrix $\Sigma$ encodes how the eight legal dimensions interact. Cases are "close" when their Mahalanobis distance is small --- when they are similar on the dimensions that matter most, weighted by the covariance structure. The legal metric also includes boundary penalties: $d(x, y) = d_M(x, y) + \sum_k \beta_k \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{boundary}_k \text{ crossed}]$.

## A.4 Parallel Transport and Holonomy

**Definition A.9 (Connection).** A *connection* on a vector bundle $E \to M$ is a rule $\nabla$ for transporting vectors along curves in the base manifold $M$. Given a curve $\gamma: [0,1] \to M$ and a vector $v \in E_{\gamma(0)}$ at the starting point, the connection produces a vector $P_\gamma(v) \in E_{\gamma(1)}$ at the endpoint.

**Definition A.10 (Parallel Transport).** The map $P_\gamma: E_{\gamma(0)} \to E_{\gamma(1)}$ that carries a vector along a curve $\gamma$ using the connection $\nabla$ is called *parallel transport*.

**Definition A.11 (Holonomy).** The *holonomy* around a closed loop $\ell$ is the transformation $P_\ell: E_p \to E_p$ obtained by parallel-transporting a vector around the loop and back to the starting point $p$. In flat space, the holonomy is trivial ($P_\ell = \text{id}$). In curved space, the vector may return "rotated."

*Legal interpretation*: Stare decisis is parallel transport on the legal manifold (Chapter 9). A legal rule established in a precedent case is "transported" along a path of intermediate cases to the current case. If the legal manifold is flat in the relevant region (the law is stable), the rule arrives unchanged. If the manifold is curved (the law is evolving), the rule arrives modified --- holonomy. This explains how precedent can evolve incrementally without being explicitly overruled: each step is locally faithful to the prior case, but the cumulative holonomy around a long path can produce substantial doctrinal change.

## A.5 Gauge Theory Fundamentals

**Definition A.12 (Gauge Group).** A *gauge group* $G$ is a group of transformations that change the mathematical description of a system without changing its observable content.

**Definition A.13 (Gauge Invariance).** A functional $J$ is *gauge-invariant* if $J(\tau(x)) = J(x)$ for all gauge transformations $\tau \in G$.

**Definition A.14 (Gauge Violation Tensor).** The *gauge violation tensor* $V_{ij}$ measures the failure of invariance: $V_{ij} = J_j(\tau_i(x)) - J_j(x)$, where $i$ indexes the gauge transformation and $j$ indexes the output dimension.

*Legal interpretation*: The equal protection gauge group consists of transformations that change protected characteristics (race, sex, religion, national origin) while preserving legally relevant facts. Gauge invariance is the mathematical statement of equal protection: legal outcomes must not depend on protected characteristics. The gauge violation tensor $V_{ij}$ measures how much a specific transformation ($i$ = race swap, gender swap, etc.) changes a specific outcome ($j$ = sentence length, conviction rate, bail amount). A legal system that satisfies equal protection has $V_{ij} = 0$ for all $i, j$. (See Chapter 8.)

## A.6 Noether's Theorem

**Theorem A.1 (Noether, 1918).** If the action functional $S[\gamma] = \int L(\gamma, \dot{\gamma}, t) \, dt$ is invariant under a continuous one-parameter group of transformations, then there exists a conserved quantity $Q$ along the equations of motion: $dQ/dt = 0$.

**Correspondence:**

| Symmetry | Conservation Law |
|----------|-----------------|
| Time translation | Energy |
| Spatial translation | Momentum |
| Rotational invariance | Angular momentum |
| Gauge invariance ($U(1)$) | Charge |
| Legal Invariance Principle | Liability conservation |

*Legal interpretation*: Chapter 9 applies Noether's theorem to the Hohfeldian gauge group. The continuous symmetries of the legal Lagrangian produce conservation laws: in a closed bilateral dispute, the total entitlement on transferable dimensions ($d_1$ through $d_4$) is conserved under adjudication. The plaintiff's gain is the defendant's loss. This is not a normative principle but a mathematical consequence of gauge invariance.

## A.7 The Scalar Irrecoverability Theorem

**Theorem A.2 (Scalar Irrecoverability).** Let $n > 1$. No continuous function $\phi: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is injective.

*Proof.* By the rank-nullity theorem, $\ker(D\phi_p)$ has dimension $\geq n - 1$ at every regular point $p$. Thus $\phi$ identifies distinct points in $\mathbb{R}^n$ that differ only along the kernel directions. The collapse is irrecoverable: no continuous function $\psi: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^n$ satisfies $\psi \circ \phi = \text{id}$. $\square$

**Corollary A.3 (Legal Verdict Irrecoverability).** A binary verdict $\phi: \mathbb{R}^8 \to \{0, 1\}$ identifies uncountably many distinct legal situations (differing on up to 8 dimensions) that receive the same output. A scalar sentence $\psi: \mathbb{R}^8 \to \mathbb{R}$ identifies distinct legal situations differing on at least 7 dimensions. In neither case can the lost information be recovered.

*Legal interpretation*: This is the mathematical foundation of Chapter 1's argument that binary verdicts and scalar sentences are structurally inadequate. The theorem is domain-independent --- it applies with equal force to GDP (economics), QALY (medicine), and IQ (cognition). The legal version simply specifies $n = 8$.

## A.8 Wilson Loops

**Definition A.15 (Wilson Loop).** Given a gauge connection $A$ on a manifold $M$ and a closed loop $\ell$ in $M$, the *Wilson loop* is the trace of the holonomy:

$$W(\ell) = \text{tr}\left(\mathcal{P} \exp\left(\oint_\ell A\right)\right)$$

where $\mathcal{P}$ denotes path ordering.

*Legal interpretation*: A Wilson loop on the judicial complex (Chapter 5) is a closed cycle in the citation network. If the Hohfeldian labels are locally consistent (each citation preserves the gauge structure) but globally inconsistent (traversing the full cycle produces a contradictory label), the loop detects a circuit split. The Wilson loop is the mathematical diagnostic for the situation where two courts, each reasoning consistently from their respective precedents, reach opposite conclusions on the same legal question.
